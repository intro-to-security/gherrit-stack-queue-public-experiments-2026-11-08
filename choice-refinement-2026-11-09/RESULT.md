# Result of the focused choice refinement

The useful reduction is a smaller dependency model for a proposed first client. It does not establish one universally best whole-system design or complete the native-stack migration. Most core distinctions in the incumbent were necessary and survive.

## What can be settled now

- **Keep one core.** Commands, a future compatible hook, direct/queued landing and observation of website activity share identity, effect custody and exact-range reconciliation. They retain interface-specific evidence; they need no separate integration engines. This was already largely an incumbent strength.
- **Separate history from its presentation.** Immutable exact ranges retain authored history; the bound comment indexes confirmed submissions. A later view can read that history. Comment edit history cannot substitute for the archive.
- **Separate authorization from request identity.** Each provider request has its own effect record. New human acceptance depends on the previously accepted scope. Use one-shot requests initially; expressly continuing authorization is an extension with validation, custody and stopping costs.
- **Separate local acceptance from remote write authority.** A retained fresh continuation can coexist with pending native work if old results and every operation's frozen inputs survive. It cannot clear old requests or authorize overlapping writes. This repairs an overly restrictive reading, rather than inventing a new permission.
- **Make staging a concrete first milestone.** Publish independently landable follow-up F alone, retain canonical B and pending B′, land F, then update B under its gate. This can defer general insertion for the staged workload. It costs serial review/landing and delayed B′ publication. It is inapplicable to authors needing joint F–B review and does not solve B's handoff.

## Which alternatives are pruned, and which are extensions

For the declared narrow domain, use explicit commands, direct calls, one cooperating publishing checkout, one-commit authoring, coupled export and a history comment. A raw mutating hook with indistinguishable dry-run input is incompatible with the desired invocation contract. Released broad CLI submit is not interchangeable under the retained export/canonical-identity protocol. Write-ahead archives remove only one CLI obstacle, while changing version/export evidence and adding bookkeeping.

Compatible hooks, a service, constrained CLI adapters, richer views, continuing automation and richer authorship remain extensions with real benefits and costs. These defaults do not dominate them for every user. General structural operations belong to the full design; the staged milestone rejects those edits before mutation and retains inputs. Selecting supported native routes/policy promises remains a release decision, followed by compatibility validation.

## What does not collapse

The main technical choice is the promise for the next overlapping hosted write. A supported exclusion contract avoids an old native write undoing newer publication. A weaker retained-intent/detected-drift contract could allow progress, but needs operation-aware recovery and may expose wrong hosted state or lose canonical review continuity. Ordinary import plus archives is insufficient: it can silently accept reversal of confirmed intent; structural overlap can make a review irreversibly merged. These are counterfactual protocol witnesses under a possible old-write premise, not observed delayed GitHub failures. Neither promise universally dominates.

The source audit also corrects the premise: direct terminal `merged` is completed-request evidence; `enqueued` completes admission. The retained post-terminal mismatches establish inconsistent reads, not a proved post-terminal ref write. Public docs support the ordinary post-merge sync/push workflow without explicitly binding the last suffix write or later-write arbitration to the UUID.

The remaining work is therefore: choose and support that operating promise; decide whether the staged domain and selected target routes/policy promises serve the intended users; and implement/validate ownership, recovery, migration and actual target compatibility. Further experiments should discriminate a selected protocol, not repeat broad quiet samples or attempt to measure away subjective priorities.

## Review and limits

Two passes compare the incumbent and alternatives under fixed C1–C8. Divergent architects, a warm adversarial critic, a new independent fresh critic for each candidate and isolated sealed-case checks supply arguments, not approval votes. Static checks verify frozen bytes, whitespace, disclosure and issue-body budget. These are design checks, not fresh production trials or measured whole-system savings.

Root read all warm, fresh and safe held-out findings in both passes. Pass1 accepted scope/default clarifications and corrected the release-profile inventory. Pass2 accepted no further conceptual-model change. After its substantive reviews and static checks, root reread in full swarm-model-refinement, model-refinement, the active tech-lead/durable coordination guidance and both fixed contracts. No further supported improvement or required check for this qualified comparison remains. This establishes local convergence of the decision model at the stated scope, not convergence on a complete operating protocol, global minimality or production acceptance. No method or C1–C8 transition occurred.

Eleven primary cases and four reserves remain unexposed to root/architects. Three partially exposed cases receive no blind credit; the incident and conservative accounting are retained. Negative/domain-boundary checks do not count as complete successful workflows. The unresolved hosted-write promise remains an adoption limit, not a solved implementation or a reason to call an indefinitely refusing pilot complete.
