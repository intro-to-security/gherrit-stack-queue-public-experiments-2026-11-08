It preserves canonical reviews, existing archive targets, distinct revision roles, operation-specific outcomes and human-owned prose. Commands, direct calls, one-shot requests and the history comment are selected defaults. Their competing benefits remain real.

## Concrete challenges to the claimed reductions

**A shared core can subsume entry points without merging their evidence.** Consider the same canonical B range arriving after direct prefix landing, queue ejection, and a website rebase. Once identity and exact ranges are established, the authored/incorporated/local inputs to integration have the same meaning. The queue admission receipt still proves admission only; the UI route may lack a UUID; the direct route can expose terminal completion. Those differences belong in capture, admission and effect/outcome handling. They do not require three range-integration algorithms. This is a constructive reason to share the importer and dispatch boundary. It does not prove one identical effect transition for all interfaces or remove the route-specific work. Candidate 002 preserves that distinction.

**The opaque-hook exclusion has a narrow impossibility argument.** Let a hook receive the same observable input in a real push and a dry run. If it sends a mutation on that input, it violates the dry-run promise. If it never sends one, it cannot perform the real-push publication promise. An explicit intent/destination contract distinguishes the cases; an adapter may obtain it and invoke the same core. This excludes the indistinguishable mutating hook under those two promises. It does not dominate every compatible hook or show that a separate preparation invocation is necessary. The candidate's narrower wording is justified.

**Local acceptance and remote settlement are independent, but immutable operation input is still necessary.** Let operation O capture checkpoint Q0 and source T, then become unresolved remotely. Coherent hosted range H permits scratch integration of T and H into fresh continuation R1/checkpoint Q1. Retain Q0 and O's input; the author can edit R1 to U. Recovery of O must still evaluate O using its frozen Q0/T, and must reload O's dispatch state under the local owner. Reading Q1 or U as O's new input would change the authorized operation. Thus Q1 can be accepted without settling O, provided it grants no overlapping hosted-write authority. The candidate preserves both required meanings rather than reducing them to one mutable “current checkpoint.” This is genuine decoupling of two permissions, with retention and attachment costs.

**F-first staging removes an immediate structural dependency, not the hosted-write question.** Start with hosted A–B and local A′–B′; old A lands. Derive F only from A's pending delta. If F is independently landable, a separate F PR against ordinary trunk can land before the amended canonical B is published. B can remain in its existing native grouping; this need not invoke unstack/parking/regrouping. But suppose the original accepted public export targets workspace ref W containing F–B′, and an older unresolved operation can write W. F's fresh managed head does not make that W write disjoint. The path needs an explicitly accepted F-only export with appropriate target scope, or it must retain the overlap barrier. Likewise, no safe B republication follows merely because F landed. Candidate 002 expressly retains these guards and the final B gate.

This construction supports deferring general insertion for the staged workload. It does not preserve simultaneous dependent review, support an inseparable F/B change, or prove that the proposed milestone can execute under current provider evidence. The ordinary trunk assumption also matters: a trunk that is another managed change's head can introduce cross-chain effects. The candidate names that boundary. If staging is used only to stop forever before B, it fails C5; the text correctly declines that success claim.

**Request identity does not require one human decision per request.** Suppose admission R0 is known to have finished, B is rewritten and ejected, and the author had expressly authorized continuing admission through a validated native-only rewrite. A new R1 can be a distinct provider request under that same accepted goal. If the author authorized only R0's exact selected head, R1 needs renewed acceptance. In either case R1 needs fresh effect identity, scope, readiness and outcome evidence. Unknown R0 cannot be retried as R1. The common authorization rule is coherent; first-client one-shot policy trades automation for narrower acceptance. It does not remove the implementation work needed to validate future continuing goals.

**Archive timing and CLI placement do not collapse into one question.** A confirmed write-ahead preservation of exact input can satisfy retention without an archive/head transaction. It needs a separate account of which attempts became confirmed submissions; allocating old-style vN for rejected attempts changes their meaning, while attempt refs plus confirmed aliases add effects and recovery. Removing archive coupling alone also does not make an open-head PR lookup equivalent to a durable canonical binding, or make a refetched lease evidence of incorporation. Conversely, none of these observations proves direct calls universally cheaper. The candidate correctly retains coupled publication as a default and keeps the changed protocol as a countercandidate.

**A stronger handoff promise has not been proved necessary, and a weaker one has not been proved sufficient.** The first-domain restriction excludes general reorder, so the candidate's indirect-merge reorder witness cannot by itself reject a same-order retained-intent recovery proposal. The candidate acknowledges that limit. Its simpler edit-loss witness remains relevant: after confirmed publication X, an older native transformation could produce H; if ordinary integration uses X as both local input and incorporated baseline, it accepts H without a conflict. Keeping an archive does not itself decide whether H is intended. A weaker protocol needs an intent-aware decision or restoration rule, plus evidence about its actual structural domain. The proposed counterfactual requires the assumed later write; it is not evidence that such a write happened after terminal direct completion.

I independently checked the live official [async API description](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/pulls/pulls#merge-a-pull-request-asynchronously): terminal `merged` is completed-request evidence, while `enqueued` is final admission evidence rather than eventual landing. The [merge guide](https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/how-tos/merge-and-close-pull-requests/merging-stacked-pull-requests) describes automatic rebasing of the next surviving PR after a merge. These are positive integration evidence. They do not explicitly specify an atomic boundary covering every suffix write and later client publication. Absence of that explicit stronger wording is not proof that normal completion is unusable, that a delayed write exists, or that every practical weaker contract must fail. Candidate 002 now keeps those conclusions separate. Retaining its strict pilot gate is a qualified risk/promise choice, not a consequence forced by C1–C8.
