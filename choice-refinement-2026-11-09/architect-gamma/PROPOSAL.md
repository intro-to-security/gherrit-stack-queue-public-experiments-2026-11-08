# Surgical baseline: keep the distinctions, narrow the promises

The incumbent's identity, revision, outcome and ownership distinctions earn their cost. The exposed evidence does not establish a need for a new orchestration architecture. It does justify removing false alternatives and separating a stronger publication promise from C1–C8. Retaining the incumbent unchanged would preserve a qualified pilot, not deliver a complete replacement: its overlapping-publication gate still stops the required repeated workflow.

My strongest surgical candidate retains the common publisher/importer and the proposed local defaults. It makes the first release's operating promise an explicit decision, offers a concrete staged path for landed amendments, and reduces placement, invocation and representation questions to the boundaries where their benefits differ. This is a proposal and comparison, not an accepted production protocol.

## What survives a surgical change

Preserve these meanings even if they share one record or implementation:

- Repository/change/canonical-PR identity and permission to overwrite are different facts. A matching SHA or trailer supplies neither adoption nor incorporation authority.
- Last authored submission, incorporated hosted range, accepted local result, captured current input and newly observed hosted range have different uses. Fetching, importing and submitting do not have interchangeable effects.
- An operation's accepted request, completed direct merge, completed queue admission, actual lifecycle/ranges and cancellation outcome are different observations. One Boolean success loses required cases.
- Durable local custody and dispatch ownership are required across recovery and transfer; neither is a provider fence. The actual dispatcher must reload authority, identity and valid retained results before an effect.
- Native membership/landing and GHerrit's input capture, archives and reconciliation have different owners. Native enrollment must exclude the legacy cascade for the enrolled scope.

These are useful distinctions, not five engines, four databases or a general scheduler. The incumbent already permits shared storage and one evaluator. Claiming that consolidation as a new simplification would overstate the change.

The local failures make the enforcement duty concrete: missing evidence recreated intent, a mismatched operation identity passed admission, stale workers dispatched twice, and a completion marker survived loss of its claimed result. A service, different file layout or more expressive state names do not repair those paths by themselves. The structural executor's nine successful after-ack recoveries are positive but narrower evidence. [Local results](https://github.com/intro-to-security/gherrit-stack-queue-public-experiments-2026-11-08/blob/740d091ca6704583d415efb714da79b33cac9352/followup-2026-11-08/local/RESULTS.md), [structural results](https://github.com/intro-to-security/gherrit-stack-queue-public-experiments-2026-11-08/blob/740d091ca6704583d415efb714da79b33cac9352/followup-2026-11-08/structure/RESULTS.md).

## The operating-promise decision

### S0: retain the incumbent's strong boundary

Retain the current rule that overlapping hosted writes wait for a supported handoff or separately supported recovery contract. This avoids claiming that a potentially superseded export is a stable basis for later operations. It is defensible for a restricted pilot and for any customer who requires a supported no-late-overwrite promise.

Its costs are material: availability loss, provider dependence and inability to complete ordinary overlapping republication. One controller, local journals and nine structural restart successes do not remove those costs. Because C5 permits a named staged restriction but rejects perpetual refusal as a replacement, S0 cannot win by treating its blocked repeated path as free. Its strongest recommendation is “retain the honest pilot boundary while specifying a useful replacement promise,” not “the full design is settled.”

### S1: retain intent and recover drift in a narrow direct-merge domain

A plausible surgical alternative changes the promise explicitly:

> GHerrit confirms captured exports and retains accepted authored intent; it does not promise that a confirmed head will never later change. Within the declared cooperating domain, later native drift is detected when status/preparation next observes it and reconciled against retained intent before accepted local work is replaced or further publication proceeds.

This has no wall-clock detection bound without a monitor. It preserves exact submitted revisions and captured local work; it does not promise capture of every transient native revision while offline. Nor does it infer server arbitration from force-with-lease.

An initial comparison domain could be: same-repository linear one-commit changes; one cooperating publisher; captured direct REST prefix merges; no queued or website operation with unresolved scope; no uncontrolled structural/head writer; and ordinary independent trunk progress permitted. Direct terminal `merged` establishes the requested prefix outcome. Coherent fetched ranges validate an import input; they are not declared a future-write fence. Any unknown dispatch still uses effect-specific reconciliation and blocks conflicting replay. Queue and website support are later named extensions, not silently treated as equivalent receipts.

S1 must retain, derive and expose three things already represented in the evidence: the native operation's captured inputs/scope, every subsequently confirmed export, and the accepted local result that must remain recoverable. A later mismatch cannot automatically become an accepted hosted deletion. The implementation must either attribute it to a retained native transformation and reapply the later authored delta using the correct predecessor, or obtain explicit recovery acceptance with the before/after intent difference visible. Ambiguous provenance stops automatic mutation and retains both inputs. Normal no-conflict operation need not ask a new question every time.

This adds a recovery rule and potentially an author decision; it is not a free deletion of S0's gate. It might provide a useful, lower-friction product than S0, but whole-system superiority is unproved. Its required acceptance work is a focused delayed-drift/capture/import protocol check, not another broad provider matrix. If it cannot preserve the published intent in the trace below, retain S0's restriction rather than call S1 complete.

The provider audit supports considering S1: documented direct completion and ordinary post-merge sync/push are real positive semantics, whereas retained mismatches establish inconsistent observations rather than a proved write after terminal success. It does not establish S1's client protocol. [Provider audit](../provider-audit/REPORT.md), pinned [merge API overview](https://github.com/github/docs/blob/8cd1075179df75319912891d6620c9cb78eefb4d/content/pull-requests/reference/stacked-pull-requests-apis-and-webhooks.md#merge-api), [management guide](https://github.com/github/docs/blob/8cd1075179df75319912891d6620c9cb78eefb4d/content/pull-requests/how-tos/create-pull-requests/managing-stacked-pull-requests.md).

## Constructive discriminating traces

### 1. Removing the gate can silently consume a confirmed edit

1. The incorporated B range H0 has `x=0`. A direct lower-prefix operation may still have an unobserved suffix effect derived from H0.
2. The author publishes X with `x=1`. Its exact archive is retained and the push is confirmed. A naive weaker design advances the incorporated baseline to X and clears pending intent.
3. A hypothetical delayed native rewrite derived from H0 yields Hlate with `x=0` on a new parent.
4. Ordinary three-way import with `I=X, L=X, H=Hlate` treats `x=0` as the hosted-side change. It can accept removal of `x=1` without a conflict.

This is a counterfactual protocol challenge, not a claim that the retained provider traces observed that delayed write. Keeping X in an archive makes bytes recoverable but does not by itself preserve the accepted intent or explain the correct next action. S1 must show a retained-intent recovery result or explicit drift acceptance; S0 avoids the overlap but retains its availability cost. A common importer can still be reused, but its baseline selection cannot be inferred from the word “current.”

### 2. Useful landed-amendment work can be staged without immediate insertion

The existing hosted fixture proves the observed F-before-canonical-B path, including executable dependence: B′ needs the field introduced by F. It does not prove settlement. [Hosted integration](https://github.com/joshlf/gherrit-native-stack-experiments-2026-11-08/blob/ade319107e71b6eff646a1574139e2c15486fe6d/local-git/hosted-integration/RESULTS.md).

There is a concrete narrower alternative to publishing F–B together:

1. Publish A–B; retain local A′/B′; land old A.
2. Import the actual hosted B into preserved scratch state. Derive new-ID F from the pending A delta. F contains no B ancestry and changes only A's intended layer.
3. With explicit author acceptance of staging, publish F alone as a fresh ordinary PR targeting the ordinary trunk. Preserve B′ locally and keep canonical hosted B at its current revision. Do not expose B′ with its missing dependency.
4. Review and land F. This is independent trunk progress, not a second grouping containing canonical B.
5. Reconcile B against the new trunk and retained B′; under the selected same-order publication promise, amend the existing B PR. Then complete another native landing/import round.

This sacrifices simultaneous dependent F/B′ review and introduces review/landing latency. It needs captures, independent public/checkpoint targets and evidence that the older operation's remaining effects cannot touch F's fresh head/PR; fresh names alone are insufficient. The completed direct prefix may supply the already-finished trunk outcome while the remaining possible suffix scope stays recorded. If actual effect scope is unknown, the trace cannot simply declare itself disjoint.

The trace does not remove the S0/S1 choice for later B publication. It does show why “the first release must support structural insertion” does not follow unconditionally from C5: a deliberately staged domain can be useful. C5 still requires explicit disposition of deliberate insert/reorder/drop/fold, and staging is a product restriction with cost. This route is a design witness requiring protocol validation, not a newly performed experiment.

### 3. Continuing authorization is different from replay

The author accepts “land canonical A/B with these authored patches using method M when the native gates permit; permit native-only rebasing.” A lands; B is rewritten and ejected. A continuing design may capture a new exact-head request only after verifying B's identity, patch allowance and actual readiness. Its new UUID/effect record refers back to the accepted authorization. An authored B edit, changed method or withdrawn authorization requires new acceptance. A one-shot design asks again even for the allowed unchanged native rewrite; that is safer against surprise at the cost of an interruption.

In both designs, withdrawing future client authorization does not cancel an admission already dispatched. Reusing the original request/receipt as if ejection had never happened fails C4. The existing explicit readmission success establishes feasibility of one new request, not preference or inherited authority. This trace preserves the useful distinction without imposing a second landing engine.

### 4. Smaller CLI code can shift burden rather than remove it

GHerrit pushes heads plus archives, then calls broad `gh stack submit`. The CLI refetches expectations and pushes branch refs again, omitting the archive vector. A later base/readiness effect can precede a refusal or missing subprocess result. GHerrit now must reconcile both publishers plus CLI version-specific semantics. Under the incumbent export promise this is worse than a true drop-in delegation.

Changing to “archive exact attempts first, then delegate publication, index only confirmed submissions” might avoid one atomic-vector requirement. It introduces abandoned-attempt/archive meaning, confirmation and recovery duties. That is a substantive competing protocol, not evidence that current broad delegation works or that atomic coupling is mandated by C1–C8. The narrow URL adapter can be evaluated separately. [Incumbent implementation comparison](https://github.com/intro-to-security/gherrit-stack-queue-public-experiments-2026-11-08/blob/740d091ca6704583d415efb714da79b33cac9352/followup-2026-11-08/refinement/candidate-003.md), [pinned adapter evidence](https://github.com/intro-to-security/gherrit-stack-queue-public-experiments-2026-11-08/blob/740d091ca6704583d415efb714da79b33cac9352/followup-2026-11-08/local/RESULTS.md).

No whole-system cost, preference or production reliability measurement is available. Stronger semantic guarantees can justifiably cost more; fewer provider premises can justifiably require more client recovery. Neither is dominance by itself.

The surgical shortlist is therefore: retain one identity/capture/import/effect core; start with explicit commands, direct calls, one cooperating checkout, separately owned history and linear one-commit authorship; eliminate the demonstrated incompatible drop-in choices; and decide S0 versus a fully specified S1 before claiming a replacement. Treat continuing authorization, service placement, richer history interfaces and richer authoring as benefits to justify under actual demand. This removes false architecture choices while preserving the remaining consequential choice and its falsifiers.
