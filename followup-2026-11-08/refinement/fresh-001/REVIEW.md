## Findings by affected layer

### 1. Evidence/comparative claim: the baseline did not make the unqualified queue conclusion attributed to it

**Candidate-only; C7.** Candidate line 11 says the follow-up “changes the earlier method-only queue conclusion.” The supplied baseline's observed-queue paragraph explicitly says “Under the tested ALLGREEN/build-one/real-CI configuration,” and ends by saying broader configurations remain untested. Its evidence table likewise names the build-concurrency-one controls and denies a universal method guarantee. Those scoped observations are not refuted by SQUASH succeeding at build concurrency two.

The follow-up supplies a useful discriminator: the additional SQUASH/build-two cells show why a method-only policy would be unsound. That is broader evidence, not a correction of an unconditional policy stated by this baseline. A reader could otherwise credit the candidate with repairing a semantic error the baseline did not contain.

Narrow the comparative claim to the actual difference: the follow-up extends the earlier build-one evidence and shows a different SQUASH outcome at build concurrency two. Candidate lines 155 and 307 can still explain why the new matrix does not support a method-only policy. This finding concerns evidence framing; it does not require reopening the range-import or publication model.

### 2. Architecture/burden claim: the evidence does not exclude orchestration that implements the required boundary

**Candidate-only; C6–C7.** Candidate line 319 says generic orchestration “would add work without resolving those failures or the provider handoff.” The inspected local report concludes more narrowly that the failures require admission, ownership, identity and completion checks at the dispatch/recovery boundary, and do not require a new architecture by themselves.

A concrete countermodel to the stronger negative claim is a shared effect runner that acquires the local owner, loads the manifest and operation, checks their identities and current phase, validates a claimed completed result, and only then calls an operation-specific dispatcher. Applied consistently, that construction can address direct-worker admission bypass, the stale `planned` worker, and the X/Y identity mismatch. Calling this runner generic orchestration does not prevent it from enforcing those checks. This is a constructive design counterexample, not evidence that such a runner has been implemented or costs less.

The candidate is right that such a runner would not create a GitHub provider handoff. The disputed sentence combines that unresolved external limitation with local failures a shared runner could address. The supported claim is that no additional general framework is required by the evidence, and no framework is sufficient merely by existing. This permits a small common boundary while leaving its implementation to a later comparison. It also avoids assigning a burden penalty to an undefined alternative.

### 3. Mapping/realization obligation: validate completion without requiring authors to journal every ordinary Git edit

**New explicit boundary, with a shared underlying obligation; C1, C4 and C6.** Candidate line 89 requires the continuation and checkpoint, “or an explicitly recorded legitimate successor,” and evidence connecting continued authoring to that successor. This is a justified response to L13, but the route from ordinary Git editing to that successor is not yet specified. The baseline already retained exact accepted results and allowed ordinary amend/rebase; it did not state this additional recovery test.

Discriminating trace: sync completes with continuation `R` at accepted commit `X`; the author selects `R` and runs ordinary `git commit --amend` or a rebase to `Y`; the process later restarts or custody transfers. The old `X` and checkpoint remain intact, but `R` now names `Y`, which need not descend from `X`, and no background hook has recorded the movement. Rejecting that state as a missing continuation would interfere with C1. Accepting any new tip without mapping review would weaken the new completion check and repeat the distinction between intended movement and damaged evidence.

This trace is not a proved candidate failure: lines 115–117 already provide explicit mapping/difference acceptance that could establish the successor, and line 324 explicitly calls for legitimate-continuation-movement validation. The remaining requirement is to connect those paths: retain `X` independently of mutable `R`, distinguish a completed historical result from the current authoring tip, and state how the next preparation/recovery records an accepted successor without a compulsory write on every Git command. Count that validation and any recovery interaction as implementation/user burden. No broader state-machine redesign follows from this finding.

## Supported changes and their limits

The received-error distinction is a concrete improvement in precision. The pinned protocol report records a 99-member create returning 504 with group 330 present, and a fresh 100-member create returning 502 with group 461 present. It also retains the pre-fix blind retry and the corrected stop rule. These discriminate a definitely rejected request from a response whose mutation outcome remains unknown. Candidate line 135 states the distinction correctly. The baseline's general unknown-outcome rules were compatible with it, but its bare “rejected” category did not make the server-error trap explicit. This is a local classification clarification supported by a real failure trace, not a new atomicity or deduplication guarantee.

The dispatch additions are also grounded. The pinned local report records L14's request count increasing from one to two after deleted operation evidence, L28's two serialized emulator requests from stale workers, and L29's admission of record Y for pending operation X. Candidate lines 59 and 89 place checks at every dispatch/recovery/transfer entry, rather than relying on a separate admission checklist. The baseline already required operational serialization and one evaluator, so much of this is concrete enforcement of existing semantics. Neither text implements the boundary; candidate lines 278 and 324 correctly leave production validation open.

The new evidence claims I checked remain bounded in the useful places. The queue paragraph distinguishes configured concurrency from delivered builds; structural restart is expressly after acknowledgement and not power loss; the four website initiations are human-attested without captured initiating receipts; multi-commit tests flatten a net range rather than preserve intermediate messages; and comment-author identity does not establish exclusive editing. The candidate does not promote these observations into provider guarantees. The five issue disposition totals add to 130, consistent with its warning that these are not 130 fresh successful trials. I did not independently check the source indexes or lossless-archive assertion.

## Preservation, useful scope and total burden

Both models retain one authored commit per logical change on a linear local branch; canonical PR bindings; immutable authored archives with historical base/tip ranges; human-owned prose; native membership and landing; explicit private/public intent; and separate authored, hosted-incorporated, accepted-local and observed states. Both preserve inputs, stop unknown overlapping effects, and keep unmentioned suffix work in scope. I found no candidate change that merges permissions, treats a fetched SHA as authority, or replaces an existing review by default.

Both also retain the same meaningful restrictions: one cooperating publisher for an affected chain, explicit preparation, fresh continuation acceptance, unsupported richer histories and shared-prefix policies, and whole-chain structural maintenance. Both describe first publication, append, amendment, prefix landing, import, F-before-surviving-B insertion, completion and adoption. Neither can yet enable the full overlapping repeat workflow under its stated guarantee. That is a shared unresolved provider boundary, openly qualified in both designs; it is not evidence favoring the baseline. The fixed contract permits a qualified design result, while C5 prevents presenting this pilot as a completed replacement.

The candidate adds implementation work for an actual local owner, reloading under ownership, binding manifests to operation identities, validating completed results and legitimate successors, and handling received mutation errors as unknown. The baseline's serialization, recovery and preservation promises already incurred much of that work; leaving enforcement unspecified did not make it free. The candidate reduces the chance that an implementer mistakes the successful cooperative fixtures for sufficient enforcement. It does not demonstrate fewer independent concepts, fewer adapters, lower maintenance cost, or lower author effort. Its proposal to store several evidence roles together is a possible representation choice; both models already allowed shared storage.

The added reports usefully narrow validation work: they replace several formerly untested premises with bounded observations and expose specific local failures to repair. They do not remove the provider handoff, production identity/adoption checks, transfer/rollback checks, or independent author/reviewer walkthroughs. The migration policy remains reversible export/handoff after pending effects resolve, preserving old reviews, archives and checkpoints; it does not promise immediate fallback or restoration of old SHAs.

The justified comparative result is therefore an evidence and enforcement clarification of substantially the same model, with two overbroad comparative/architecture claims to narrow. Full-workflow enablement and whole-system simplification remain unestablished for both candidates. The coordinator retains layer diagnosis, acceptance, required rereads and stopping decisions.
